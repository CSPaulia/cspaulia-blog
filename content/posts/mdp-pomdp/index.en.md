## 1. Markov Models

### 1.1. The Markov property

> **Definition 1 (Markov property)**: the state at the next time step depends only on the current state, not on the earlier history:
>
> \[P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, s_{t-1}, \ldots, s_1) = P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t)\]

This is also called the "memoryless" property: all influence of the past on the future is absorbed into the present.

### 1.2. The Markov model family

Along two dimensions — whether the state is fully observable, and whether actions are considered — Markov models fall into four classes [1]:

| | No actions | With actions |
|---|---|---|
| Fully observable state | Markov chain (MC) | Markov decision process (MDP) |
| Partially observable state | Hidden Markov model (HMM) | Partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) |

The relations between the four can be drawn as one picture: the Markov chain is the common origin, branching left into the MDP and right into the HMM; the MDP plus the HMM gives the POMDP; and the MDP and the POMDP belong to reinforcement learning while the HMM does not.

The observability dimension is what decides whether the model has a latent variable. Seen on one and the same two-state chain, the difference is immediate: in a Markov chain the states \(S_1, S_2\) are visible directly, whereas in a hidden Markov model they are hidden and only the observations \(O_1, O_2\) they emit can be seen.

<figure>
  <img src="../../../posts/mdp-pomdp/markov-chain-vs-hmm.png" alt="One two-state chain, seen as a Markov chain and as a hidden Markov model" width="100%" />

Source: Berkeley CS188 slides (redrawn).</figcaption>

- **Markov chain (MC)**: a random process whose probabilities depend only on the current state; there is no decision making.
- **Hidden Markov model (HMM)**: a Markov model whose state is not fully observable — only the observation sequence generated by the hidden states can be seen; it contains no decisions.
- **Markov decision process (MDP)**: a decision problem on top of a Markov chain. The next state is random, but its probability depends on the current state and the current decision.
- **Partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP)**: combines the ideas of MDP and HMM — decisions are made, but the decision maker cannot directly observe the true state.

## 2. Markov Models in Reinforcement Learning

### 2.1. Background

Reinforcement learning describes the interaction between an **agent** and an **environment** [2]:

1. The agent observes the environment's state \(s_t\);
2. The agent selects an action \(a_t\) according to its **policy**;
3. The environment transitions to a new state \(s_{t+1}\) and returns a **reward** \(r_t\);
4. Go back to step 1, until the episode ends.

<figure>
  <img src="../../../posts/mdp-pomdp/agent-environment-loop.png" alt="Agent-environment interaction" width="80%" />
  <figcaption>Figure 3: The agent executes actions; the environment returns new states and rewards. Source: Sutton &amp; Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, Fig. 3.1 (redrawn).</figcaption>
</figure>

> **Definition 2 (Policy)**: given a state \(s\), outputs a probability distribution over actions \(\pi(a \mid s)\). The policy is the agent's "brain".

> **Definition 3 (Return)**: the discounted sum of future rewards starting at time \(t\):
>
> \[U_t = R_t + \gamma R_{t+1} + \gamma^2 R_{t+2} + \cdots = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \gamma^k R_{t+k}\]
>
> where \(\gamma \in [0, 1)\) is the discount factor: the smaller \(\gamma\), the less future rewards matter. Taking \(\gamma < 1\) is also what makes the infinite series above converge.

> **Definition 4 (Action-value function)**: given policy \(\pi\), the expected return of taking action \(a\) in state \(s\):
>
> \[Q_{\pi}(s, a) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[ U_t \mid S_t = s, A_t = a \right]\]

> **Definition 5 (State-value function)**: given policy \(\pi\), the expected return in state \(s\):
>
> \[V_{\pi}(s) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[ U_t \mid S_t = s \right]\]

The goal of reinforcement learning: find the optimal policy \(\pi^{*}\) that maximizes the expected return:

\[
J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \gamma^{t-1} r(s_t, a_t) \right], \qquad
\pi^{*} = \arg\max_{\pi} J(\pi)
\]

### 2.2. Markov Decision Process (MDP): Fully Observable States

Reinforcement learning can be described by a Markov decision process (MDP) \(\langle S, A, P, r, \gamma \rangle\) [1].

> **Definition 6 (Markov decision process)**: the 5-tuple \(\langle S, A, P, r, \gamma \rangle\):
>
> - \(S\): the set of states
> - \(A\): the set of actions
> - \(P\): the state transition probability function \(P(s' \mid s, a) = \mathbb{P}(S_{t+1} = s' \mid S_t = s, A_t = a)\)
> - \(r\): the reward function
> - \(\gamma\): the discount factor

The transition probability also satisfies the Markov property — the next state depends only on the current state and the current action, not on the earlier history:

\[
P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, \ldots) = P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t)
\]

Unrolled over time as a graphical model, the dependencies become explicit: the action \(A_t\) is determined by the state \(S_t\) (the agent decides from the current state), the reward \(R_t\) is determined jointly by \(S_t\) and \(A_t\), and the next state \(S_{t+1}\) is likewise determined jointly by \(S_t\) and \(A_t\).

### 2.3. Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP): Partially Observable States

A partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) combines the ideas of MDP and HMM: as in an HMM, the current state cannot be observed directly — a latent variable exists, or the state is only partially observable; as in an MDP, the agent must make decisions and maximize rewards [1]. In practice most problems are POMDPs, e.g. autonomous driving (sensors can only perceive a small part of the environment) and robot navigation.

- Agent observes the entire environment \(\rightarrow\) **MDP**
- Agent only observes a part of the environment \(\rightarrow\) **POMDP**

> **Definition 7 (Partially observable Markov decision process)**: the 7-tuple \(\langle S, A, T, R, \Omega, O, \gamma \rangle\):
>
> - \(S\): the set of states
> - \(A\): the set of actions
> - \(T\): the state transition probability \(T(s' \mid s, a)\)
> - \(R\): the reward function \(R: S \times A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\)
> - \(\Omega\): the set of observations
> - \(O\): the observation probability \(O(s', a, o) = P(o \mid s', a)\) — the probability of observing \(o\) after taking action \(a\) and transitioning to \(s'\)
> - \(\gamma\): the discount factor

Unrolled over time, the seven components give the full interaction between agent and environment in a POMDP:

<figure>
  <img src="../../../posts/mdp-pomdp/pomdp-agent-environment.png" alt="Agent-environment interaction in a POMDP" width="100%" />
  <figcaption>Figure 5: Agent-environment interaction in a POMDP. (1) The agent acts according to its history, \(a \in A\); (2) the environment state transitions, \(s \rightarrow s'\); (3) the agent receives an observation \(o \in \Omega\) and a reward \(r \in \mathbb{R}\); (4) the agent updates its history \(h_t \rightarrow h_{t+1}\) and its belief \(b_t \rightarrow b_{t+1}\). Note step 4: unlike an MDP, what the agent receives is not the state but an observation, so it must decide from the history \(h_t = (a_1, o_1, \ldots, a_{t-1}, o_{t-1})\) and the belief \(b_t(s) = \Pr(s \mid h_t)\). Source: Berkeley CS188 slides (redrawn).</figcaption>
</figure>

What the POMDP adds over the MDP is exactly the HMM part: an **observation model**. In the graphical model this shows up as one extra edge from a state to an observation.

<figure>
  <img src="../../../posts/mdp-pomdp/pomdp-graphical-model.png" alt="The POMDP as a graphical model" width="100%" />
  <figcaption>Figure 6: The POMDP as a graphical model. Compared with the MDP it has one extra edge, \(S \to O\) (dashed violet) — the sensor model. All the agent sees is the observation \(O\), never the true state \(S\). Source: Berkeley CS188 slides (redrawn).</figcaption>
</figure>

Since the true state is not visible, the agent instead maintains a **belief state**: a probability distribution over the current state, \(b(s)\). Upon each new observation, the belief is updated by Bayesian filtering:

\[
b'(s') \propto O(o \mid s', a) \sum_{s \in S} T(s' \mid s, a)\, b(s)
\]

> A POMDP can be converted into a continuous-state MDP over the belief space: the belief \(b\) itself serves as the "state". This is why the POMDP is seen as a natural extension of the MDP.

### 2.4. Summary: Where MDP and POMDP Sit in Reinforcement Learning

**The MDP is the standard form of a reinforcement learning problem.** Almost all RL theory is built on it: the Bellman equation, value iteration, policy iteration, Q-learning, policy gradients. They take the shape they do because the Markov property buys two guarantees: the value function can depend on the current state alone, written \(V(s)\), instead of the whole history; and a time-independent optimal policy \(\pi^{*}(a \mid s)\) exists. Without the Markov property neither holds, and the theory immediately becomes intractable.

**The POMDP is the norm in the real world; the MDP is its idealization.** Sensors are noisy, the field of view is occluded, and the state is never fully measured — real problems are almost all POMDPs.

**A POMDP is not a "harder MDP" — it is estimation and decision making stacked on top of each other.** This is what the model-relationship diagram in §1.2 means at the algorithmic level: the Markov chain describes how the state evolves, the HMM solves how to infer the state from observations, and the MDP solves how to choose actions; POMDP equals MDP plus HMM, that is, the coupling of **decision making** and **estimation**. That coupling is exactly why it is hard — the two cannot be solved separately: the quality of the estimate bounds the quality of the decision, and the decision determines what observations are available next.

**The MDP and the POMDP are the foundation and the shared language of reinforcement learning, not the whole of it.** The rest of this series — [Deep Reinforcement Learning Overview]({{< ref "/posts/deep-reinforcement-learning-overview" >}}), [DQN]({{< ref "/posts/dqn" >}}), [PPO]({{< ref "/posts/ppo" >}}), [RLHF]({{< ref "/posts/sft_rlhf" >}}), [RLVR]({{< ref "/posts/rlvr" >}}) — proposes algorithms and settings choices that hang off this skeleton.
